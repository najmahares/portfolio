PRAXIS

Learning to invest by actually doing it, without risking real money

PRAXIS is a simulation-first learning platform that solves a problem every new investor faces: you cannot learn to invest from reading alone, but the only way to learn by doing is to risk money you cannot afford to lose. PRAXIS removes that tradeoff. Learners work through a 131 lesson curriculum, build a virtual portfolio using real companies at real prices, and reason through outcomes with an AI mentor named Jema that adapts to their progress. The market layer currently supports the Nairobi Securities Exchange, but the core idea, practice investing before you practise with real money, applies to any market.

Overview

PRAXIS is a practice environment for the stock market, built for people who have never invested before. It replaces passive financial education, reading articles and watching videos and memorizing definitions, with something closer to a flight simulator: a safe space where you make real decisions, see what happens, and learn from the outcome without risking a shilling.

The core loop is Learn, Practice, Decide, Reflect. Each cycle ends with the learner explaining why they made a decision, the system showing what actually happened, and Jema, an AI mentor designed to ask better questions rather than give answers, helping them connect the choice to the outcome.

The product is deliberately not a brokerage, not an investment advice platform, and not a generic finance course. Every screen reinforces that this is simulated, educational, and free of risk. The first market integrated is the Nairobi Securities Exchange, because that is where the clearest gap exists today, but the platform is designed so additional markets can be added without changing the learning model.

The Challenge

Someone learning to invest today faces a real gap. They can study every beginner's guide and still freeze the moment a real decision appears. Reading about risk is not the same as experiencing it. The distance between "I understand what a P/E ratio is" and "I know whether this stock is worth my money" is exactly where most beginners stop.

This problem is not specific to one market. It is universal. First time investors everywhere learn the same way: they lose money, they get scared, and many of them never come back. The lesson costs them and teaches them the wrong thing.

Existing tools do not close this gap. Trading simulators assume you already know the vocabulary. Financial literacy apps test recall rather than build judgment. Brokerage demo accounts can reinforce poor instincts because nothing meaningful is at stake. General AI assistants answer "what should I buy?" directly, which is the opposite of what a beginner needs.

PRAXIS starts from a different premise. The learner makes the decision, explains their reasoning, and sees the result. The mentor's job is to ask the questions that surface what the learner missed. That reflection is the mechanism through which judgment, not just information, is built.

The Solution

PRAXIS is a complete learning platform: landing page, authentication, application shell, dashboard, learning journey, practice library, market explorer, virtual portfolio, progress view, bookmarks, notifications, community, and a full AI mentor experience.

The curriculum spans 131 lessons across 18 levels, organized into three tiers: Foundations (eight levels covering how markets work, how to read a company, valuation, risk, and portfolio construction), Asset Deep Dives (five levels on stocks, bonds, funds, FX, and commodities), and Universal Skills (five levels on investor direction, thesis writing, psychology, best practices, and international investing). Each concept is taught through short lessons that lead into practice scenarios, drills, and missions built around real companies at real prices.

The market layer currently runs against the Nairobi Securities Exchange, with live company data and real pricing. It is deliberately pluggable. The learning model, the practice engine, the mentor, and the portfolio simulation are all market agnostic. Adding a second market means adding a data adapter, not rebuilding the product.

The mentor, Jema, is what makes the product distinctive. It streams responses from Groq, renders financial concepts as live Mermaid diagrams, generates illustrations on request, and offers adaptive quizzes pulled from lesson content. Every conversation runs through an extraction pass that identifies durable facts about the learner and stores them so future conversations can recall what matters.

Above all, PRAXIS is honest about what it is. The mentor will not tell you what to buy. It will not predict prices. Every screen carries a safety line and every simulated trade is clearly labeled.

Engineering Deep Dive

The mentor pipeline is the technically interesting piece. A user message triggers a chain: loading recent durable facts about the learner from Postgres, building a system prompt that combines a fixed persona with dynamic context (current page, progress, holdings), streaming a completion from Groq, and then a second silent extraction request that pulls durable facts from the turn and persists them.

The rendering pipeline avoids the usual LLM chat pitfalls. Assistant responses are parsed by a hand written markdown renderer that supports a strict whitelist and produces React elements directly. No raw HTML injection on the assistant's own text. Diagrams render with Mermaid's strict security mode. Images are proxied through a server route so the browser never touches the AI provider directly.

Security was treated as a first class concern from the first commit. Every API route re validates input, sessions live in HttpOnly cookies with a custom revocation layer, and every external provider call has a timeout and friendly error mapping. Platform events log every authentication, admin, rate limit, and community action with severity, IP, and user agent for later review.
